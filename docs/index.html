Ariadne

Small language models that know where to look, instead of trying to know everything.

The Ariadne project

View the repository Read the architecture

Abstract

Language models freeze what they learn at training time, so their knowledge goes stale and they cannot tell when it has. Ariadne takes the opposite approach. It is an offline knowledge service, built from curated open libraries and served to any model through the Model Context Protocol, plus a team of small models trained to search that service, cite what they find and say when they find nothing. A router sends each question to a few domain specialists in parallel, an aggregator keeps only the claims it can verify, and a control plane shares one modest machine fairly between administrators and users. Knowledge lives outside the weights, where it can be updated, audited and owned. The same structure extends to private knowledge: a company connects its own documentation and gets a small model of its own that already knows how to look things up, without retraining it on every change.

1The problem with knowing everything

Today's assistants answer from memory. Their facts are fixed at a training cutoff, compressed into billions of parameters, and impossible to inspect. When a library changes its API or a guideline is revised, the model keeps answering confidently from an old copy. Making models larger postpones the problem; it does not remove it.

Ariadne starts from a different premise: a model does not need to contain the library, it needs to know how to walk through it.

In the myth, Ariadne gives Theseus a thread so he can find his way through the labyrinth without memorising it. The project applies the same idea to language models: the knowledge is the labyrinth, kept outside the model, and the model learns to follow the thread to a cited answer.

2System overview

Ariadne is split along one clear line. A control plane runs on a server with plenty of storage and memory, and holds everything that has state: the knowledge, the sessions, the decisions about which model runs where. Inference nodes are machines with a capable GPU that only serve models over HTTP. They do not know about each other; adding one means registering its address.

Inside the models everything is in English. Questions are translated on the way in and answers on the way out by a local LibreTranslate service, which is far cheaper than asking a language model to work in every language. Code, citations and identifiers are masked before translation and restored afterwards.

3A knowledge service in three levels

The first part of Ariadne is useful on its own, for any model, including large hosted ones. It turns a library of offline archives into a linked, searchable corpus and serves it through a single MCP server with a handful of tools: search, read, neighbors, translations, feedback and sources.

The archives come from the Kiwix catalogue: Wikipedia, dozens of Stack Exchange sites, official programming documentation, medical references, survival and repair manuals. In the first phase they are downloaded by hand, in batches by size, and Ariadne simply watches a folder; later the dashboard can fetch them from the catalogue directly.

4From raw archives to a curated corpus

Raw web content is not safe to train on or to hand to a model as-is. It can hide instructions aimed at language models, carry encoded payloads, or include commands that would be dangerous if copied. Ariadne treats the archives as raw material and cleans everything at the entrance, so nothing downstream has to defend itself.

  • Flags catch injection patterns ("ignore previous instructions", system-style tags, hidden text), long encoded blocks, dangerous commands such as piping a download into a shell, and clinical doses.
  • The fidelity check compares the facts of the original and the rewrite. If the curator changed a number or a claim, the article does not pass.
  • Updates are incremental. When a new archive version arrives, each article is compared by fingerprint; only changed articles are curated again, and removed ones are archived, never deleted.
  • Attribution travels with the text. Rewritten Wikipedia and Stack Exchange content keeps its original licence and credit.

Rewriting an entire encyclopedia this way would take years on one machine, so curation follows use: what people actually consult is curated first, and very large batches can be sent to rented GPUs.

5Indexing by use, linking by default

Vectorising every article on modest hardware is impractical, so Ariadne does not try. The semantic level behaves like a cache that warms with use: whatever is consulted gets embedded, and documents that are consulted often pull their neighbours in after them.

heat(t) = heat(t0) · 2−(t − t0) / h + w(1)

Each access adds a weight w (more when a result is actually used in an answer than when it is merely shown), and the score decays with a half-life h. When a document crosses a threshold, its neighbours are queued within a daily budget: adjacent sections, internal links, articles in the same topic and the nearest neighbours in vector space, one hop at a time.

The guiding rule is that no content stays isolated. Articles are linked across archives, so a history answer can point to a primary source in Wikisource, and across languages, so an event can be read in the language where it happened; for historical questions the original-language source is offered alongside the translation. Every link records its origin and confidence, and can be reviewed.

6The life of a question

Speed is not the goal; consistency is. A question may take seconds or minutes, as long as every statement in the answer can be traced to a source. Figure 4 follows one question through the whole system.

7Routing the team of small models

The router does not generate text. It is based on open-jev, an open reconstruction of a typed decision model, trained from scratch on Ariadne's own data. In a single call it answers several typed questions about each message, each with a calibrated probability, so a score of 0.30 means roughly a 30% chance that the domain is relevant.

Routing policy

  1. Call every domain that scores 0.30 or more.
  2. Always call at least the top-scoring domain.
  3. If the top score is below 0.50, the router is unsure, so add the general specialist as well.
  4. Call at most three specialists per question to protect shared resources; administrators may have a higher cap.
  5. Read the session's recent history, so a conversation that moves from planting crops to an irrigation script calls both domains.

These values are starting points, tuned on a validation set. Routing errors are absorbed twice: when unsure the router calls more specialists, and each specialist is trained to refuse questions outside its domain, so a wrong call ends in a clean refusal instead of a contaminated answer.

8Specialists, code and the aggregator

All specialists share one small base model kept in memory, with a lightweight LoRA adapter per domain. An adapter weighs a few dozen megabytes and swaps in under a second, so a single machine can host a whole team. Each specialist sits behind its own OpenAI-compatible HTTP endpoint, and all of them search the same Ariadne service: knowledge lives in one place, and machines only add reasoning capacity.

Code generation

A model with a few hundred million parameters writes weak code, so code generation has its own base model, StarCoder2-3B, with one adapter per language: Python, JavaScript for Node, JavaScript for the browser, and Go, with more to follow. The base stays resident; switching from Python to JavaScript means swapping an adapter, and the router starts loading it as soon as it detects the language. Generated code always runs in a disposable sandbox before it reaches the answer. Compiling and passing tests is both the check and the reward signal for training.

One contract between all parts

Every specialist answers in the same structure. Citations are attached to individual claims rather than to the whole answer, so the aggregator can drop one unsupported sentence without discarding everything else.

// specialist → aggregator
{
  "status": "answered | refused | partial | error",
  "refusal_reason": null,
  "answer_en": "…",
  "claims": [{
    "text": "Resting heart rate in adults is usually 60–100 bpm.",
    "citations": [{ "doc_id": "wiki/health/heart-rate", "revision": "r1842", "quote": "…" }]
  }],
  "confidence": 0.78,
  "trajectory": ["search …", "read …"],
  "model": { "base": "SmolLM2-360M", "adapter": "health", "adapter_version": "v3" },
  "latency_ms": 4200
}

A partial status lets a specialist say what it knows and admit what it does not, which is always better than inventing. Each request carries a deadline; the aggregator proceeds without any specialist that misses it.

The aggregator

The aggregator is a larger model from the same family, SmolLM2-1.7B, sharing the tokenizer and training pipeline of the specialists. It verifies every cited passage exactly, then checks that the passage supports the claim; groups claims that agree; resolves contradictions by preferring the source most specific to the domain, or shows both positions with their sources; and writes the final answer only from what survived. If nothing survives, it says so.

9Administrators, users and sessions

One machine is shared by an administrator and a few users at once, each possibly switching subjects in the middle of a conversation. Two rules make this work. First, models keep no state: conversations, history and context live in the control plane, and a harness such as Hermes only sends a session identifier. Second, memory is a budget with reserved lanes, managed by the model manager.

  • Administrator. Has a reserved slice of memory, is never refused, and can pin or unload any model from the dashboard.
  • Users. Can load a new model only if it fits in free memory. Otherwise the request waits in a queue or is served in degraded mode by the general specialist, but it is always answered. Requests from several users are batched, each with its own context cache.
  • The trainer user. A system account whose questions exist only to produce training data. Its trajectories can be published; real users' data never leaves the server. A good question from a real user can be asked again by the trainer, reworded and stripped of personal details.

10Keeping the machine healthy

On a machine with unified memory, running out of memory does not just slow models down; it slows the operating system itself. Ariadne protects each node with a watchdog that is always running and that wakes up when something leaves its normal range.

  • Deterministic, not clever. The watchdog's core is a set of plain rules, not a model. If the machine is in trouble, a model may be too, so the safety net cannot depend on one. A small model can sit on top to explain incidents in the dashboard.
  • Local and independent. It runs as a separate system service on each node and survives even if the rest of the stack fails or the network drops in the middle of a heavy load.
  • What it watches: memory pressure, swap, temperature and queue latency.
  • What it does: unloads cold models, pauses training, blocks new loads and restarts stuck workers.

The control plane decides what is desirable; the watchdog guarantees that nothing crosses a physical limit. If a node disappears, the control plane marks it offline and keeps answering in degraded mode with search results alone.

11Training, evaluation and lineage

The models are trained on a Mac Studio with MLX. Rented or free cloud GPUs (Kaggle, Colab, RunPod, Vast.ai) can be switched on by the administrator for heavy runs; those use PyTorch, and the resulting adapters are converted back. Training checkpoints often, because free GPUs disappear mid-run.

Curating the training data

  1. Only trajectories whose citations passed verification are eligible.
  2. A quality signal is required: positive feedback, or a high score from the judge. Without one, a trajectory stays in quarantine.
  3. Real questions weigh more than synthetic ones, and synthetic data has a capped share.
  4. Answers with negative feedback are kept as negative examples for reinforcement learning.
  5. Nothing from the golden test set, or close to it, ever enters training; duplicates are checked automatically.
  6. Every adapter knows exactly which trajectories trained it, so problematic data can be traced and removed.

Three layers of evaluation

Deterministic checks come first because they are cheap: the citation exists and the passage is in the document, the code compiles and passes its tests, the specialist refused when the question was outside its domain. A strong model then acts as judge for correctness, completeness and grounding, against fixed criteria; this is used only to validate the first prototype, not as a permanent part of the system. Finally a person reviews a sample, especially where the judge and the rules disagree.

Everything visible in the dashboard

The dashboard records the full lineage: for each training run, the pipeline, the data used, test results, what failed and why; for each question, its trajectory, its citations, whether it passed verification and which runs used it. It also shows the curation state of every article, which models are loaded where and in what state, memory per model and per session, the queue, and every watchdog incident.

Backups and public releases

Everything lives on the home server, under its own backup policy. Once a week, an encrypted external release is published to GitHub in parts of at most 2 GB, with a manifest, fingerprints and a restore script: the curated wiki, adapters, trainer data, the approved-model registry and configuration. Real users' data is never included, and base model weights and archives are left out because they can be downloaded again.

12Safety by construction

  • Injection stopped at the door. Content is cleaned when it enters the wiki. Retrieved text is always passed to models inside clear delimiters, marked as data, and attack examples are part of training and testing.
  • Models do not act. Specialists only search and answer. Anything with an effect, such as running code, goes through the system, never directly through a model.
  • An isolated sandbox. Each execution runs in a disposable container with no network, a read-only disk, and strict limits on time, memory and processes, on a different machine from the models.
  • Health is held to a higher standard. Every health article is curated, every health claim needs a medical source and high confidence, the system says plainly when it does not know, and answers note that it is not a substitute for a professional. When a question shows signs of urgency, such as chest pain or difficulty breathing, the first line tells the person to seek medical help immediately.

13A model of your own

The open libraries are a starting point, not the destination. Because the models learn to search rather than to memorise, the same structure works for knowledge that belongs to you.

  • Connect the places where your knowledge already lives, such as Confluence, Azure DevOps wikis or Notion, as new sources next to the public archives.
  • Start from a base that already knows how to search, cite and refuse, trained on the public corpus.
  • Train a small specialist on your own material: smaller than a general model, and sharper in your field.
  • Keep it current without retraining. When your documentation changes, the model reads the new version on its next search.

The result is an assistant that runs on your own hardware, answers from your documents with citations, and never sends them anywhere else.

14Design principles

  • Offline first. The system works without internet. Cloud GPUs are an optional boost for training, never a dependency.
  • Every answer is traceable. Claims cite a specific revision of a specific document.
  • Validate before building. Each layer is built only after the one before it has proved its worth.
  • Models are replaceable. Knowledge, training data, evaluation sets and pipelines outlive any single model, so moving to a larger one reuses all of them, and a small model can stay on as a fast planner beside a larger one.
  • Provenance for models too. Only models on an approved list run, each stored with its origin and original licence, passed on unchanged. Adapters inherit the licence of their base. The project is for non-commercial use.
  • Nothing ships untested, and everything that happens is visible in the dashboard.

16The hypothesis to test first

Phase 2 answers one question: does a small model that searches Ariadne answer better than the same model alone? It compares three variants on a few hundred Python questions: the base model without search, the base model with search, and the model trained to search. There is no router, no aggregator and no cloud. Answers are scored by automatic checks (citations exist, code runs) and by a stronger model acting as judge, with a person reviewing a sample.

If the trained variant clearly wins, the rest of the architecture is a matter of growing from there. If it does not, the project learns that in weeks rather than after building everything else.
